Verb To Be

Learn how to use the verb to be in affirmative, negative and interrogative sentences.

beginner children teens speaking writing

1. Introduction

The verb to be is one of the most important verbs in English. It is used to identify people, describe characteristics and express states.

Its main forms in the present tense are: am, is and are.

2. Affirmative Sentences

Use am with I, is with he, she and it, and are with you, we and they.

Examples: I am a student. She is happy. They are teachers.

Dica: para iniciantes, enfatize as formas contraídas I'm, you're, he's e she's.

3. Negative and Interrogative Sentences

To make negative sentences, add not after the verb: I am not, she is not, they are not.

To make questions, place the verb before the subject: Are you happy? Is she a teacher?

4. Practice Activity

Ask students to introduce themselves using the verb to be. They should say their names, ages, characteristics and feelings.

Example: My name is Ana. I am 12 years old. I am Brazilian. I am happy.

5. Learning Material

Use a song with the expressions I am, you are and he or she is. Encourage students to repeat the sentences and associate them with pictures or people in the classroom.
